DESIGNED FOR TWO MACS

Just point. Then type.

Choose which Mac provides input and which Mac receives it. No main window, no screen streaming, and everything stays a click away in your menu bar.

Your familiar keyboard & trackpad.

Keep typing on your MacBook with native function keys, pointer movement, clicks, dragging, and continuous two-finger scrolling that respects natural scroll direction. Optional experimental native multi-finger gesture forwarding sends gestures directly to the target Mac without hard-coded shortcut hacks.

A mouse that stays yours.

New in 1.6

Prevent automatic lock.

Optionally hold a process-scoped macOS display assertion while connected so an idle MacBook won’t lock mid-session. Global Lock Screen settings stay untouched; quitting releases it immediately.

Direct, encrypted, no cloud.

Install the exact same lightweight Swift app on both Macs. Bilateral 6-digit pairing pins peer identities and encrypts input directly—no accounts, no relay servers, and no silent Wi-Fi fallback.

Fail-safe by design.

Disconnects, sleep, lid close, or stale heartbeats immediately stop interception and release held keys. Brief scheduling gaps recover the route automatically without tearing down your session.

Physical validation scope: verified over a direct cable on an M1 Pro MacBook → M4 Mac mini (both macOS 15.8.1) covering keyboard, trackpad, native multi-finger gestures, and source input isolation. Other Mac models, macOS versions, and apps remain unverified.

TRANSPORT

Choose your connection.

Pick your transport manually in the menu bar. KeyBridge never silently falls back to Wi-Fi or switches links behind your back.

Direct Thunderbolt

Recommended for daily work · physically validated

Best experience

Connect two Macs directly with a certified Thunderbolt 3, 4, or 5 USB-C cable to form macOS Thunderbolt Bridge. If buying a USB4 cable, verify explicit Thunderbolt compatibility and Mac-to-Mac Thunderbolt networking—“100W”, “full-featured”, or “4K” alone is not enough.

  • Lowest latency for typing and trackpad tracking
  • Supports experimental native multi-finger gestures
  • Supports experimental wired microphone sharing

Bluetooth

No cable required · backup mode

Wireless backup

Connect two Macs wirelessly when you don’t have a Thunderbolt cable handy. Bluetooth has noticeably higher input latency—especially on the trackpad—so it is best kept as a temporary or backup link.

  • Typing, pointer, clicks, dragging, and 2-finger scroll
  • Higher latency than direct Thunderbolt
  • Microphone audio is not supported over Bluetooth
HARDWARE TIP · MAC MINI (2024)

Use a rear Thunderbolt port on Mac mini M4 / M4 Pro.

The two front USB-C ports on Mac mini (2024) are USB 3 (10 Gb/s) and cannot establish Thunderbolt Bridge. Plug your Thunderbolt cable into one of the three rear Thunderbolt ports (Thunderbolt 4 on M4, Thunderbolt 5 on M4 Pro).

EXPERIMENTAL · WIRED ONLY

Your microphone, on the other Mac.

Share one Mac’s microphone over the encrypted Thunderbolt link. Off by default, with synchronized readiness switches on both Macs.

Enable Share microphone below the native-gestures toggle. KeyBridge checks your connection, permissions, and optional receiver component step by step. Both switches stay off until capture and audio reception are both active—and switching off either end stops sharing immediately.

  • 48 kHz mono PCM in 20 ms frames over encrypted wired transport (Bluetooth unsupported)
  • Receiver virtual driver installs once with admin approval and reloads locally—no Mac reboot required
  • Select KeyBridge Microphone in your voice app; never alters your system default microphone

Experimental status: physical two-Mac voice-app acceptance testing is still pending. Reconnecting never resumes capture automatically.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Honest answers on cables, ports, gestures, security, and beta boundaries.

Report an issue on GitHub ↗
Does any USB-C cable work?

No. USB-C only describes the connector shape. Prefer a USB-C-to-USB-C cable explicitly rated for Thunderbolt 3, 4, or 5 and plug it into Thunderbolt ports on both Macs. For USB4 cables, confirm Thunderbolt compatibility and Mac-to-Mac Thunderbolt networking. Wattage, “full-featured”, or display specs alone do not prove compatibility.

Which port should I use on Mac mini?

On Mac mini M4 / M4 Pro, use a rear Thunderbolt port (Thunderbolt 4 on M4, Thunderbolt 5 on M4 Pro). The two front USB-C ports are USB 3 (10 Gb/s) and cannot establish macOS Thunderbolt Bridge.

Which trackpad gestures work?

Movement, clicks, dragging, and continuous two-finger scrolling work out of the box. Native multi-finger gesture forwarding is experimental and off by default. It was user-confirmed over a direct cable from an M1 Pro to a Mac mini M4 (macOS 15.8.1), forwarding native events for the destination system and apps to interpret without fixed shortcut mappings.

What if the MacBook locks while I am using the other Mac?

Turn on Prevent automatic lock in KeyBridge. While connected, KeyBridge holds a process-scoped macOS display-sleep assertion so idle time does not lock the Mac before your next input. Turning it off or quitting KeyBridge releases it immediately. Manual lock, lid close, and system sleep still pause forwarding; global Lock Screen settings are never modified.

Does it use Wi-Fi?

No. KeyBridge uses either direct Thunderbolt Bridge or Bluetooth, chosen manually. It never falls back to Wi-Fi or silently switches transports.

Can I return to local input with a mouse?

Yes. External mouse input stays local. Click the menu bar icon with your mouse to select this Mac, stop sharing, or reconnect—or press Control–Option–Command–Escape at any time.

Why does macOS warn me when opening the beta?

This beta uses a local development signature and is not notarized by Apple, so macOS may block first launch. If you trust the download, open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Do not disable Gatekeeper globally.

What is sent between the Macs?

Keyboard and trackpad input travels directly between paired Macs through an authenticated, encrypted session. Optional microphone audio is sent only when enabled over the wired link. Screen video is never streamed. Diagnostics exclude typed text and raw gesture payloads, though they may include device names and local network details.
