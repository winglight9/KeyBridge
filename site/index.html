MADE FOR TWO-MAC DESKS

Pick a Mac. Start typing.

No extra keyboard cluttering your desk, no screen streaming, no messy main window. Just pick which Mac to control from the menu bar.

Feels just like your own keyboard & trackpad.

Type normally with native function keys, click, drag, and scroll smoothly with two fingers in the exact direction you are used to. You can also turn on experimental multi-finger gestures—your swipes and pinches are handled natively by the other Mac, not faked with keyboard shortcuts.

Smooth 2-finger scroll Native Fn keys Multi-finger gestures (Beta)

Your mouse always stays yours.

KeyBridge never takes over your external mouse. You can always use it to switch back, reconnect, or stop sharing—and if you ever want an instant keyboard escape, one shortcut brings control right back.

Instant return ⌃⌥⌘⎋
New in 1.6

No more mid-work auto-lock.

Turn on “Prevent automatic lock” so your idle MacBook doesn’t go to sleep and lock while you’re typing on the other Mac. It never changes your System Settings, and stops the moment you switch it off or quit.

Direct, encrypted, no accounts.

Install the exact same small app on both Macs. Confirm a 6-digit code on each screen to pair, and everything travels encrypted straight between the two Macs—no cloud servers, no login, and no sneaky Wi-Fi fallback.

Never gets your keyboard stuck.

Unplug the cable, close the lid, or put a Mac to sleep and KeyBridge immediately lets go of all keys and returns input locally. If the link hiccups briefly, it pauses safely and reconnects automatically.

Real-world test note: verified daily over a Thunderbolt cable on an M1 Pro MacBook → M4 Mac mini (both running macOS 15.8.1), including multi-finger gestures and external mouse isolation. Other Mac models and macOS versions have not been fully tested yet—feedback is welcome.

HOW THEY CONNECT

Cable for speed. Bluetooth for backup.

You pick the connection mode manually. KeyBridge won’t quietly switch behind your back or fall back to Wi-Fi.

Direct Thunderbolt Cable

Best for daily use · feels instant

Recommended

Connect the two Macs directly with a certified Thunderbolt 3, 4, or 5 cable. If you buy a USB4 cable, double-check that it explicitly supports Mac-to-Mac Thunderbolt networking—labels like “100W”, “full-featured”, or “4K” aren’t enough.

  • Snappy, near-native feel for typing and trackpad
  • Supports experimental multi-finger trackpad gestures
  • Supports experimental microphone sharing

Bluetooth

No cable needed · handy backup

Wireless backup

Great when you don’t have a Thunderbolt cable on hand. There is noticeable cursor lag compared to a cable, so it’s best kept for quick tasks or emergencies.

  • Works for typing, pointer, clicks, dragging, and 2-finger scroll
  • Higher delay than a direct Thunderbolt cable
  • Cannot share microphone audio
QUICK TIP · MAC MINI (2024)

On Mac mini M4 / M4 Pro, plug into the back.

The two USB-C ports on the front of the 2024 Mac mini are regular USB 3 ports—they look the same, but they don’t support Thunderbolt networking. Plug your cable into any of the three Thunderbolt ports on the back instead.

EXPERIMENTAL · CABLE ONLY

Borrow your MacBook’s mic on the other Mac.

Using a Mac mini without a mic? Turn on one switch to send your MacBook’s microphone over the encrypted Thunderbolt cable. Off by default.

Turn on “Share microphone” right under the gestures option. If a permission or driver is missing, KeyBridge walks you through it. Both Macs show it active only when audio is actually flowing, and turning it off on either Mac stops it immediately.

  • Clear, low-latency 48 kHz audio over the encrypted Thunderbolt cable (no Bluetooth audio)
  • One-time virtual mic install on the receiving Mac—activates right away with no reboot
  • Just pick “KeyBridge Microphone” in your call app; your system default mic stays untouched

Heads-up: this feature is still experimental and hasn’t been tested across every voice app yet. Reconnecting never turns your mic back on automatically.

GET STARTED

Up and running in 3 steps.

Install the same app on both Macs, allow permissions once, and confirm a 6-digit code.

  1. 01

    Install on both Macs

    Download KeyBridge v1.6 and drag it into Applications on both Macs first (so macOS doesn’t ask for permissions twice). Built for Apple Silicon Macs on macOS 14+.

    /Applications/KeyBridge.app
  2. 02

    Grant permissions once

    Allow Input Monitoring & Accessibility on the Mac you type on, and Accessibility on the Mac you control. Allow Local Network (wired) or Bluetooth when prompted.

    Input Monitoring Accessibility
  3. 03

    Pair & pick your target

    Check that the 6-digit code matches on both screens and approve. Choose your keyboard under Device and target Mac under Apply to—both Macs stay in sync automatically.

    6-digit pairing code
First-launch macOS warning

Because this free open-source beta isn’t paid-notarized by Apple yet, macOS will warn you the first time you open it. Just go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Never turn off Gatekeeper globally.

FAQ

Common questions.

Plain answers about cables, ports, gestures, privacy, and what to expect in beta.

Ask or report a bug on GitHub ↗
Can I use any USB-C cable?

No. Most regular USB-C charging or display cables can’t create a Thunderbolt network link. Get a certified Thunderbolt 3, 4, or 5 cable (or a USB4 cable explicitly confirmed for Mac-to-Mac Thunderbolt networking) and plug it into Thunderbolt ports on both Macs.

Which port should I use on Mac mini?

On Mac mini M4 / M4 Pro, always use one of the three Thunderbolt ports on the back. The two USB-C ports on the front are USB 3 only and won’t work for Thunderbolt Bridge.

Which trackpad gestures work?

Moving, clicking, dragging, and smooth two-finger scrolling work out of the box. Multi-finger gestures (like switching desktops or Mission Control) are available as an optional beta toggle and work over Thunderbolt—tested on M1 Pro → Mac mini M4 (macOS 15.8.1). They are handled natively by the other Mac, not faked with fixed shortcuts.

What if my MacBook locks while I’m controlling the other Mac?

Turn on “Prevent automatic lock” in KeyBridge. While the two Macs are connected, it temporarily keeps the screen from sleeping and locking on its own. Your system Lock Screen settings are never changed, and closing the lid or manually locking still works as usual.

Does it use Wi-Fi?

No. It only uses a direct Thunderbolt cable or Bluetooth, whichever you pick. It never falls back to Wi-Fi.

Can I switch back using my mouse?

Yes. Your external mouse always stays on its own Mac—just click KeyBridge in the menu bar to switch back or stop sharing. You can also press Control–Option–Command–Escape at any time.

Why does macOS show a warning the first time I open it?

KeyBridge is a free, open-source beta without a paid Apple Developer ID notarization yet, so macOS blocks the first launch by default. After checking the source or release page, open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Don’t turn off Gatekeeper globally.

Is my typing private? What gets sent?

Everything travels encrypted directly between your two paired Macs—never through the cloud. Screen video is never sent, and microphone audio is only sent if you explicitly turn it on over a cable. Diagnostic logs never record what you type.
