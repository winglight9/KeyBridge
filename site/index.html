Your familiar keyboard.

Keep typing on your MacBook. Share its built-in trackpad for movement, clicks, dragging and two-finger scrolling, with optional experimental native multi-finger forwarding.

A mouse that stays yours.

Your external mouse keeps working locally. Use it to choose a destination, reconnect, or return input to this Mac.

One small app. Both Macs.

The same app sends and receives input. No main window, no screen streaming, and no Wi-Fi fallback.

An earlier build was physically validated over a cable on an M1 Pro and Mac mini M4, both running macOS 15.8.1: keyboard, trackpad, multi-finger gestures and source input isolation worked. Other models, systems and apps remain unverified.

Your microphone. On the other Mac.

New in 1.2 · Experimental wired microphone sharing. Off by default, with separate enable and mute controls.

Audio travels through the paired, encrypted wired connection. Bluetooth audio is not supported. The receiving Mac needs an optional virtual-microphone component, installed once with administrator approval; macOS may require you to restart it to load the component. No restart happens silently.

Choose KeyBridge Microphone in your voice app. KeyBridge does not change the system default microphone. Disconnecting stops capture; reconnection does not resume it. Physical two-Mac acceptance and compatibility with individual voice apps remain unverified.

connection.

A direct Thunderbolt connection gives you lower input latency. Bluetooth connects without a cable, with a higher input delay.

ThunderboltRecommended daily · physically validated›

Use a direct cable that establishes macOS Thunderbolt Bridge. Prefer a USB-C-to-USB-C cable explicitly rated for Thunderbolt 3, 4 or 5. For USB4 cables, also confirm Thunderbolt compatibility and Mac-to-Mac Thunderbolt networking; “full-featured”, “100W” or “4K” alone is insufficient. On Mac mini M4 / M4 Pro, use a rear Thunderbolt port. The front USB-C ports cannot provide this app’s Thunderbolt connection.

BluetoothNo cable required›

Connect two Macs without a cable. Bluetooth has higher input latency, which is more noticeable when moving the pointer. Choose your connection from the menu.

Ready on both Macs.

Install the app on each Mac and grant the required permissions.
Then pair and choose which Mac will control the other.

  1. 1

    Install the same app

    Download KeyBridge and move it to Applications on both Macs. The current beta is for Apple Silicon Macs running macOS 14 or later.

  2. 2

    Allow the required permissions

    On the source Mac, grant Input Monitoring and Accessibility. On the destination Mac, grant Accessibility. Both Macs need Bluetooth access when using Bluetooth.

  3. 3

    Pair, then choose your destination

    Choose a connection mode, compare the pairing code on both Macs and approve it on each. Select the source under Device and the destination under Apply to; the route syncs to the other Mac, so you only choose once. Use your external mouse to stop forwarding or return input locally.

Frequently asked questions.

Does any USB-C cable work?›

No. USB-C describes the connector. Prefer a USB-C-to-USB-C cable explicitly rated for Thunderbolt 3, 4 or 5 and connect it to Thunderbolt ports on both Macs. For USB4, additionally confirm Thunderbolt compatibility and Mac-to-Mac Thunderbolt networking. Charging wattage, “full-featured” or display resolution alone does not establish compatibility. Enable Thunderbolt Bridge on both Macs. The app reports link, address and discovery status, but a missing connection alone cannot prove that the cable is faulty.

Which port should I use on Mac mini?›

On Mac mini M4 / M4 Pro, use a rear Thunderbolt port: Thunderbolt 4 on M4, or Thunderbolt 5 on M4 Pro. The two front USB-C ports are USB 3 ports. They have the same connector shape but cannot establish the Thunderbolt Bridge this app needs. Connect the other end to a Thunderbolt port on your MacBook.

Which trackpad gestures work?›

Movement, clicks, dragging and two-finger scrolling are available. Native multi-finger forwarding is still experimental and must be enabled in the menu. In an earlier build, the user confirmed multi-finger operation over a direct cable from an M1 Pro to a Mac mini M4, both on macOS 15.8.1. This does not establish compatibility with every system and app. Native gesture events are interpreted by the destination system and app according to their settings, without fixed shortcut mappings.

Does it use Wi-Fi?›

The app offers Bluetooth and direct Thunderbolt networking. It does not fall back to Wi-Fi or silently switch between connection modes.

Can I return to local input with a mouse?›

Yes. External mouse input stays local. Open the menu bar app with your mouse and select this Mac as the destination, stop sharing, or reconnect. The app also returns input locally when its connection lease expires.

Why does macOS warn me when opening the beta?›

This early beta uses a local development signature. It is not signed with a Developer ID or notarized by Apple, so macOS may block its first launch. If you trust this download, follow the release’s instructions and use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway when available. Do not disable Gatekeeper globally.

What is sent between the Macs?›

Keyboard and trackpad input travels between paired Macs through an encrypted session. Optional microphone sharing sends audio only when enabled over the wired connection. Screen video is not streamed. Pairing compares a code on both devices. This beta has not had an independent security audit; diagnostics can include device names and local network information.
